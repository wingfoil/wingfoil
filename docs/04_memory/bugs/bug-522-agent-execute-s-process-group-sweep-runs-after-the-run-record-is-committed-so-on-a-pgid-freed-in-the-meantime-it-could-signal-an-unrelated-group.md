## Summary

`task-355` (bug-516) makes a headless `agent execute` run deliver its forwarded and time-bound signals to the agent's
process group and, once the agent has exited after a signal, send `SIGKILL` to what is left of that group
(`src/agent/launch.ts`, `SignalTakeover.sweep()`). The sweep runs in `launchAgent`'s `finally`, after the run's lookups
and the commit of the run record, which can take tens of seconds after the agent exited. If every member of the group
has exited by then, its pgid is free, and an unrelated process could in principle have become the leader of a group
with the same id: the sweep would then send it `SIGKILL`.

## Steps to Reproduce

1. On `main` after `task-355` merges: `grep -n "signals.sweep()" src/agent/launch.ts` → the call in `finally`, after
   the record commit.
2. Read `sweep()`: it signals the pgid it recorded at the spawn, with no check that the group still holds a process of
   the run.

## Expected Behavior

The sweep signals only a group that still belongs to the run: it is skipped when an earlier delivery to the group
returned `ESRCH` (the group was already empty), or it runs before the slow lookups, or spec-016 §3.3 step 15 states the
residual risk and why it is accepted (Linux does not reuse a pid while it is a live pgid, and `pid_max` is large).

## Actual Behavior

The sweep sends `SIGKILL` to the recorded pgid whenever a signal reached the agent, however long after the agent exited.

- Found by `task-355`'s reviewer (2026-11-09, follow-up finding 1), introduced by the fix of bug-516; the risk is
  negligible in practice, which is why it is low.
- Related: bug-516, spec-016 §3.3 step 15.
