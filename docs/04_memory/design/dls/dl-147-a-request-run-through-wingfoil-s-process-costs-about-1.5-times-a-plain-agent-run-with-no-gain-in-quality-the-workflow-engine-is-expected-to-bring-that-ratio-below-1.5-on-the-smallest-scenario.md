## Context

**The first published campaign of the WingFoil benchmark measured what WingFoil's process costs.**
The benchmark compares the same coding agent on the same scenarios with and without WingFoil. Its
release v0.1 ran campaign `c82a5e74885b`, execution 2, with `claude-sonnet-5`, against WingFoil
v0.2.2 (`12537b62`), and exported one finding for WingFoil, metric **M-K1 (cost)**:
- finding note (permalink, tag v0.1):
  <https://github.com/wingfoil/wingfoil-benchmark/blob/v0.1/findings/c82a5e74885b-2-s1-1.0-m-k1-baseline+wingfoil.md>;
- results, category C (development):
  <https://wingfoil.github.io/wingfoil-benchmark/c82a5e74885b/2/category-c.html>.

- **Where the extra cost goes:** mostly cache reads. On S1 the wingfoil runs read 9.0–10.8 M
  cached tokens each, the baseline runs 2.9–10.0 M. That is the process at work: reading rules and
  decisions, and filing a task and decision-logs for every request.
- **No quality gain on S1:** M-Q1, the share of the scenario's public hidden tests passed on the
  final snapshot, averaged over the runs, is 98.4% for wingfoil (164, 158 and 165 of 165) and 99.8%
  for the baseline (164, 165 and 165 of 165), a difference within the runs' variability.
- **What the process is.** The wingfoil arm follows its operating manual
  (<https://github.com/wingfoil/wingfoil-benchmark/blob/v0.1/arms/wingfoil/manual.md>): one task per
  request, one decision-log per design decision, each submitted and approved. WingFoil itself does
  not require that granularity; it is the process a WingFoil project is set up to follow, run by
  hand because the workflow engine does not exist in v0.2.2 (no `workflow start`, no phase
  execution).
- **What the benchmark also measured**, in WingFoil's favour and recorded there, not here: S2's
  checks, S3's recorded review and continuity, S8's rule compliance.

## Decision

**WingFoil treats the cost of its process as a measured property, and holds the workflow engine to
reducing it.**
- The working hypothesis is the benchmark's **H1** (calibration v0.1, §11,
  <https://github.com/wingfoil/wingfoil-benchmark/blob/v0.1/docs/calibration/v0.1.md>): the engine
  lowers the process's overhead, because an agent stops spending turns and context on finding and
  re-reading the process.
- **Target:** wingfoil ÷ baseline cost (M-K1) **below 1.5× on S1**, and falling on at least three
  of the four scenarios.
- If the next campaign misses the target, a follow-up decision-log weighs process proportionate
  to the request — lighter ceremony for small requests (for example no decision-log per minor
  design choice), or role context loaded selectively — before more process is added.

## Rationale

- **Cost without quality gain on small work is the adoption risk** `dl-140` (D2) names: Alex, the
  solo developer, is reached through light templates and will not pay 1.5–2× per request for the
  same result.
- **The overhead is the process, not the tool.** Cache reads dominate, and the manual's
  per-request granularity is what drives them; an engine that hands the agent its phase, role and
  context should cut exactly that.
- **An external, repeatable measure** keeps the claim honest, in line with `dl-131`: the cost is
  reported, and the target is checked by a campaign, not asserted.
