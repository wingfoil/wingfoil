## Context

`memory submit` moves an element into a gated state (`task`: `draft → pending`) and commits its content as it was
left; the approver's `memory approve` then passes the gate. Nothing ties what is approved to what was submitted.

- **What happened.** In both by-hand Journey 4 runs of `task-242`'s AC 3 (2026-10-15, recorded under `### AC 3 — by
  hand (approver, 2026-10-15)` in `task-242`'s Execution Notes), the agent launched by `agent execute --next` wrote
  the task and ran its own `memory submit`, and then restructured the `pending` task's body with a plain
  `docs(task): …` commit: no reject, no `memory amend`, no new submit. The approver then approved content other than
  what was submitted. `memory history` lists that commit as an entry with operation `null` and `pending → pending`,
  and no check refuses or flags it (follow-up item 21 of the W3 B8 batch).
- **What exists.** `memory amend` (`dl-108`) records a correction to a document as an amendment, with a `Reason:`
  and the approver's authority, and `memory.yaml` declares `task` `amendable: true`. The governance check
  (`scripts/check-governance.cjs`, `git-conventions` §9) refuses a status change outside a `wf()` operation
  (`dl-139`), not a body change.
- **Why it matters.** A gate is evidence that the approver accepted a stated content (P1.7). If the content can
  change between submit and approve without trace at the gate, the approval record (`Approver:`, `Reason:`) refers
  to a text the approver may never have read, and two runs of the same step can reach the gate with different
  content, against the Determinism Index.

## Decision

Choose one:

- **(A) Frozen.** A `pending` (gated) element's body is frozen after its submit: changing it needs a `memory reject`
  back to `draft` and a new submit, or a declared `memory amend` by the approver. A plain commit that changes the
  body of an element in a gated state is refused by the governance check, as `dl-139` refuses a status change, and
  `memory approve` refuses when the committed body differs from the one the last submit committed.
- **(B) Allowed and surfaced.** Edits stay allowed, and the gate shows them: `memory approve` (and `workflow
  status`'s pending approvals) name every commit that changed the body since the last submit, so the approver
  approves knowing it; `memory history` labels such a commit as an edit while gated rather than an operation `null`.
- **(C) As today.** No rule; the approver reads `git log` before approving.

**Recommendation:** (A) for agents (what is approved is what was submitted, which the record can prove), with
`memory amend` as the declared route for a correction the approver wants; (B)'s surfacing as the fallback if (A)
proves too strict in practice.

## Rationale

- An approval should be reproducible from its record: the submit commit holds the content, the approve commit
  accepts it. A free edit between them breaks that link silently.
- The tools for a declared change already exist (`memory reject`, `memory amend`); (A) only makes the undeclared
  route visible to the checks.
- (B) keeps flexibility for human authors but leaves the agent's bootstrap free to edit after submit, which is what
  happened.

## Actions

1. Ratify (A), (B) or (C), or reject to `draft`. Owner: approver, at v0.4 `release-planning`.
2. Candidate actions: for (A), the governance rule and the approve-time check (`scripts/check-governance.cjs`,
   `src/memory/`), and the `agent execute` bootstrap stating that a submitted element is not edited; for (B), the
   approve-time and `memory history` surfacing; `spec-001` or `spec-008` amended accordingly.
