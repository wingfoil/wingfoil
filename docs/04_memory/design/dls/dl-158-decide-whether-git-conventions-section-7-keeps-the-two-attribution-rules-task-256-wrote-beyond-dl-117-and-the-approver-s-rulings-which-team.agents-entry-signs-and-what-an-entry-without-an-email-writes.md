1. **Which entry signs when there are several.** "When `team.agents` declares more than one entry, it is the

## Decision

Options, for each rule:

- **Rule 1 (several entries):**
  - **(a) Ratify a precise form**: the signing entry is the one `agent execute` launches (its `adapter`
    resolves it, `spec-016`); a hand session uses the entry whose `name` is the agent's own, and with no such
    entry it signs with the first entry and says so in the commit body.
  - **(b) Remove it** and leave §7 single-agent until `agent execute` defines the selection.
- **Rule 2 (no email):**
  - **(i) Ratify it**: no `Co-Authored-By:` without an address; `AI-Model:` alone records the AI part.
  - **(ii) Replace it**: an entry used for commits must declare an email (the schema or the directive refuses a
    signing entry without one), so Q1 (B)'s co-authorship is never waived.
  - **(iii) Remove it**, leaving the case undefined.

**Recommendation: Rule 1 (a), Rule 2 (ii).** (a) keeps the sentence but makes it pick exactly one entry, which
is what a trailer needs; (ii) keeps `dl-117` Q1 (B) whole, and an email is already optional only because an
agent may be declared before it signs anything.

## Rationale

- A directive clause with no ratified source is the case `dl-117` itself was filed to end ("a trail nobody
  declared cannot be audited"); ratifying or removing both clauses restores the trace from §7 to decisions.
- Rule 1 as written is ambiguous by construction (`executes_as` is a list of roles shared between entries);
  (b) is honest but leaves multi-agent projects without a rule just when `agent execute` ships.
- Rule 2 (i) is cheap and fires nowhere today (this repository's entry has an email); (ii) costs one
  refinement in `AgentEntry` or one directive sentence, and keeps the policy's "every commit" promise.
