## Context

**Routing and authority disagree.** A workflow phase may declare `approval: { by_role: R }` (`spec-003`), and
since v0.3 `workflow next` and `workflow status` route the pending approval to the members holding `R` (P4.14,
`task-225`). But `memory approve` and `memory reject` check only the `approver` role: `spec-017` §5.1 says
routing "does not change who may decide" and flags a routed member without the role with the warning
`W_APPROVAL_ROLE_WITHOUT_AUTHORITY`; its OQ-5 closes the question the same way.

**Reproduced on 0.3.2** in a throw-away Kanban repository with a member whose only role is `reviewer`:
`wingfoil memory reject <a pending task> --reason "needs work" --format json` → `user not authorized to approve type
'task'`, exit 1. A consumer (the WingFoil UI) reports, on 0.3.1, the whole loop: a decision-log gate routed
`by_role: reviewer`; `workflow next` and `status` name the reviewer and print the warning once per step and member;
`workflow list` reports no diagnostic; the reviewer can neither approve nor reject; an approver the gate is not routed
to approves it. So a team that routes review gates to reviewers must also make each reviewer an approver, which grants
every other gate too.

`dl-162` (`ready`, v0.4) decides whether the stakeholder persona approves and rejects; it does not change the
`approver`-only rule for the verbs.

## Decision

Options:
- **(a) The routed role may reject; only `approver` may approve.** `memory reject` accepts a member who holds the
  role a pending approval of an open run routes the gate to; `approve` keeps the `approver` check. A reviewer can
  send work back, never pass it.
- **(b) The routed role decides both ways** for the gates the workflow routes to it; `approver` keeps authority
  everywhere else.
- **(c) Keep the rule and refuse the routing**: a `by_role` naming a role that cannot decide is an error at
  `workflow list` (and every workflow read), not a warning per pending step.

**Recommendation: (a), with (c)'s check for `approve`-only gates.** Sending work back loses no authority (the element
returns to a state the author resubmits from), so it is safe to give the routed role; passing a gate stays with the
role REQ-SEC-03 names. A routing that can then do nothing at all (a role that may not even reject, e.g. on a type with
no reject target) is refused when the workflow is read, so the mismatch is seen once, at configuration time.

## Rationale

- **Reject is the reviewer's natural act** in a review gate; today it needs a second person.
- **Approve stays narrow**: REQ-SEC-03 and adr-006 keep the forward edge with `approver`; `dl-212`'s agent refusal
  applies unchanged, since the shared check is where it lives.
- **(b) widens authority** per workflow file, which a project's contributor can edit without an approval (see the
  decision-log on non-gate authority, `dl-220`).
- **(c) alone** keeps the duplication of roles the consumer reports.
